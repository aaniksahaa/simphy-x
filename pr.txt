When I quoted SimPhy code earlier, I had downloaded files from the public upstream repository at https://github.com/adamallo/SimPhy into my scratch directory. That directory has since been cleared, so nothing of it remains locally except the fresh clone I just made for this check.

It is the right source, with one caveat about versions. Upstream has three tags, and v1.0.2 was committed in November 2015, four months before your binary's March 2016 build date. The paper's own file paths also say SimPhy_1.0.2. Master has eight later commits from 2016 to 2020 that change trees.c by over a thousand lines, so master does not match your binary exactly. The v1.0.2 tag is the reference to use.

I verified the match against the binary itself. Your binary is unstripped and carries debug info, so it still contains function names. All four functions I cited are present. Disassembling the expected-leaves function shows precisely the quoted computation: call the tree-height function, multiply by the rate difference, call exp, multiply by the leaf count. That formula is identical at trees.c line 8559 in v1.0.2 and in master, so nothing I told you about the dup-rate theory depends on the version difference.

Can you build and optimize it? Yes, in principle:

gcc, make and the GSL development package are already installed here.
Three development packages are missing but available through apt: libmpfr-dev, libgmp-dev and libsqlite3-dev. Your binary has those libraries statically linked, which is why it runs without them.
My recommendation is to keep the official 1.0.2 binary for the actual datasets, since the paper used it and reproducibility matters more than speed there. SimPhy's cost is dominated by the multispecies coalescent root-finding in MPFR, and in your pipeline the AliSim and FastTree phases dominate wall time anyway for large runs. Optimizing SimPhy is worth it only if profiling shows the --upto true phase is your bottleneck.
